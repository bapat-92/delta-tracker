Delta filter (applies to the recommendations)

Keeps sold strikes inside a delta band. Delta is about the chance the option finishes in the money, so 15–25 delta is a strike with roughly a 15–25% chance of being hit at expiry. It filters the Strike finder, strike candidates, Inverted Batman and the Adjustment planner (new sold legs). It does not change your own legs.

Public prices only, so no API key is needed. If you get "Failed to fetch", the API may block browser requests (CORS): put a proxy URL in the API base. Entry price is the premium per 1 BTC you got or paid. BTC per lot is an assumption, so check the contract specs.

Order list (for you to place on Delta)

Turns the changes in the What-if card into an order list: safe order of execution, limit prices from live quotes with a slippage cap, and new sells sized to fit your margin budget. It holds no keys and sends nothing. You place the orders on Delta yourself.

Load a plan into the What-if card first (from the planner or the call guard).
Batman adjustment trigger (alert only)

On each side the payoff climbs in a straight line from your bought strike up to the hump at the sold strike. This watches BTC and alerts when it is halfway along that climb (the trigger), so you can adjust early. It never places orders.

Off.
Adjustment planner (next 0.5% move)

Checks the odds, then for a 0.5% fall and a 0.5% rise tries combinations of keeping, rolling or closing each side, and ranks the three best by the widest profit range at expiry. Planning only: it places no orders.

Uses your current legs, the live chain for their expiry and the expiry time in the strike backtest card (UTC).
Call-side guard (alert only)

Watches the calls you sold. Fires when BTC reaches your trigger price, when a sold call is worth a set multiple of what you collected, or when BTC gets close to a sold strike. It shows what buying the calls back costs right now (at the ask). It never places orders.

Off.

Day-by-day strike backtest

For each past day: take BTC at your entry time, pick the call and put strikes at each move distance, look up what those daily options traded for at that time, and see what selling them to expiry would have made or lost.

Inverted Batman: best strike combinations

Sell more of the outer call and put, buy fewer of the inner ones (your 5,000 sold / 1,500 bought structure). It tries many strike combinations from the live chain and ranks them by the BTC price range at expiry where profit stays at or above your minimum share of margin. A second group below shows three more options where the call and put are the same distance from spot.

Uses the leverage and BTC-per-lot boxes above. Buys are priced at the ask and sells at the bid.
Not-inverted Batman: back-ratio spreads

The opposite shape to the Inverted Batman. On each side you sell fewer options close to the price and buy more further out, so a big move either way pays and the loss sits in a pocket between the strikes. It tries every strike pair and lot ratio and keeps the ones with the best profit if BTC stays that also keep the pocket loss inside your cap.

Uses the funds from the Inverted Batman box (or Available balance), and the leverage and BTC-per-lot boxes above.
Move trigger (alerts only, places no orders)

Each time BTC moves by the step from the last trigger price: a rise suggests selling the call, a fall suggests selling the put, at the strike priced nearest your target premium.

Estimate = short lots × BTC per lot × spot ÷ leverage. Max profit and max loss use your entry prices and lots, assuming everything is held to expiry (position P&L at expiry is worked out from the strikes). It ignores margin scaling for large sizes, offsetting between legs and fees, and I haven't confirmed Delta applies this formula to short options. The order preview on Delta shows the real requirement, so use that.

Premium value is lots × BTC per lot × option price, in USD. You collect it when you sell; it rises as the options gain value. The trigger is the premium value at which your loss equals the stop-loss (entry value + stop-loss). With the net basis, bought legs are subtracted from both the entry and the current value. Based on mark prices, so it lags and doesn't place any order.
